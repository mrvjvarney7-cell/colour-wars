How to Play

The board

7×7 grid. Players take turns placing dots on cells. Colours & opponents are chosen on the setup screen - any mix of human and AI seats.

Opening move

Your very first move of the game may target any empty cell (or one you already own) and places 3 dots at once, instead of the usual one. This is what gets games moving quickly on a 7×7 board - without it, the opening phase would take far longer to reach any real contact between players.

Later moves

Every move after your opening must target a cell you already own, placing a single dot. You cannot place on an empty cell or an opponent's cell directly - the only way to gain new territory is by exploding into it.

Critical mass & explosions

Every cell explodes once it reaches 4 dots - corners, edges and interior cells alike, regardless of how many neighbours they actually have. An exploding cell loses all 4 dots and sends one dot to each orthogonally adjacent cell (up to 4: up/down/left/right), capturing that cell for the exploding player's colour. A corner cell only has 2 neighbours and an edge cell only 3 - dots that would go to a neighbour that doesn't exist are simply lost.

Chain reactions

If a cell gains a dot and that pushes it to 4 or more, it explodes too - on the very next wave, simultaneously with any other cell that also just reached critical mass. This can cascade through many cells in a single turn, flipping large sections of the board to one colour. The chain preview (hover a legal move on desktop, long-press on mobile) shows exactly which cells a candidate move would detonate before you commit to it.

Elimination & winning

Once every player has made their opening move, any player reduced to zero cells is eliminated. The last player with any cells left wins. In a 3-4 player game, elimination can happen well before every other player has had many turns - a single well-placed chain can wipe someone out early.
